## Deliverable A — `docs/04-learnings.md` (~2,500–3,500 words)

"What we learned building an agentic shopping assistant from scratch". Must be **specific, first-person
and grounded in this build** — not generic AI-agent advice. Cover, with concrete detail from this repo:

1. **The build process** — how six research agents + three build agents ran in parallel against a written
   contract (`demo/CONTRACT.md`: schema + module signatures + file ownership). What worked (no merge
   conflicts, contract caught API drift), what broke (a VM restart killed two agents; the LLM model
   catalogue changed and the retries failed — the *contract* survived, the *agents* didn't), and the
   rule we would adopt: **agents own files, humans own interfaces**.
2. **Retrieval lessons** — why hybrid (BM25 + dense + co-purchase graph → RRF) beat either alone; why
   RRF over score blending; the cross-encoder rerank's contribution; the cold-start problem; why
   deterministic constraint parsing beat asking the LLM for filters (latency, cost, reproducibility).
3. **Grounding lessons** — the difference between "a chatty answer" and "an answer whose every claim has a
   citation"; how we enforce it (citation gate + claim verifier re-reading the DB); the failure mode where
   a *templated* answer can still be wrong.
4. **Guardrail lessons** — budget caps, stock truth, refusing to recommend without evidence, and the
   principle: **guardrails belong in middleware, not in the prompt**, because prompts are advisory.
5. **Data lessons** — what we used from public datasets and what each was actually good for (catalogue
   from Amazon 2023, real user history from 5-core ratings, real transactions from Olist, gold labels
   from ESC); the limits (no MRP, no order stream for Amazon, US-only prices); the cost of ingestion on
   a 4-vCPU box (embedding throughput ~40 texts/s → 80 min for 195k products) and what we'd do differently
   (quantised ONNX, embed a slice, or precompute in a batch job).
6. **Evaluation lessons** — how to measure an agentic shopping surface without a real user base:
   retrieval metrics on the ESC gold set, groundedness, task success, latency/cost; which metrics are
   proxies vs real; the traps (a metric that improves because you shrank the catalogue).
7. **What we would do next** — 30/60/90-day roadmap for the prototype, and the three things we would
   delete if we had less time.

## Deliverable B — `docs/05-moats.md` (~2,000–2,800 words)

"Where the moats are in agentic commerce" — a rigorous, citable map. Ground it in
`research/02-competition-landscape.md` (cite it and the primary sources it lists) and in what *this*
prototype could and could not do. Must include:

1. A **moat matrix** by type: proprietary catalogue/inventory, transaction history, review corpus,
   fulfilment/logistics, distribution/traffic, interface habit, evaluation data, trust & safety,
   multi-sided network effects, and *agent-readable commerce surfaces* (APIs/protocols).
   For each: who holds it, how strong, how defensible over 3 years, and whether a startup can own it.
2. The **honest asymmetry**: which moats Amazon/Shopify/Google hold that a startup structurally cannot

   evaluation layer) rather than compete head-on.
3. **Five concrete wedges** a small team can attack, each with: the wedge, why incumbents won't copy it

4. **The disintermediation trap**: what happens when the agent becomes the interface and the marketplace
   becomes the plumbing — who captures the margin, why Amazon blocked external agents while selling its own,

5. **The "trust as product" argument** — with evidence from the research (fake reviews, counterfeit risk,
   price-history opacity, the reported failure modes of Amazon's assistant).
6. A **self-assessment of this prototype**: which moat does it actually touch, and which it pretends to.
